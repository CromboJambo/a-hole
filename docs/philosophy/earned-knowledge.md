# Earned Knowledge

## The Core Concept

**Your knowledge is your most durable asset.**

Every time you solve a problem for yourself, you're creating something that can't be taken away from you. It's your knowledge. It's your understanding. It's your earned wisdom.

This is what a-hole is about: **capturing and preserving your earned knowledge as mods.**

## The Vendor's Approach

When vendors collect telemetry, they do it to:

1. **Identify pain point** — "Users are struggling with X"
2. **Build feature that addresses symptom** — "We'll add feature Y"
3. **Sell upgrade** — "Feature Y is in Pro version Z"

The vendor sells you a feature. You pay for it. You use it. If you stop using it, you've lost your money.

## Your Approach

When you collect telemetry (with a-hole), you do it to:

1. **Identify pain point from your own mirror** — "I struggled with X"
2. **Realize the solution is a config change, an alias, a one-line script** — "I can fix this with setting A"
3. **Log that solution as earned knowledge** — "I've solved this problem"
4. **Share it as a mod on the CM platform** — "Here's how I fixed it"

You solve the problem yourself. You document it. You share it. If you stop using it, you still have the knowledge. You still have the mod. It's yours forever.

## What Is Earned Knowledge?

Earned knowledge is:

**The things you figure out for yourself.**

Not what someone told you to do. Not what the documentation says. Not what the vendor recommends. The things you discovered by trial and error, by experimentation, by persistence.

Examples of earned knowledge:

- "I found that setting X to Y makes this tool work the way I want"
- "I discovered this workaround for this vendor's design flaw"
- "I created this alias that saves me 10 seconds every day"
- "I customized this config to match my workflow"
- "I learned that this pattern works across multiple machines"

## The Mod as Knowledge

A mod is just a named diff with a manifest. But it's also:

- A record of a problem you solved
- A solution you documented
- A pattern you discovered
- A workflow you optimized

Every mod is a piece of earned knowledge. Every mod is a piece of you that can't be taken away from you.

## Solving the Real Problem

The key insight: **solving the real problem is cheaper and better than buying the vendor's solution.**

What vendors sell you:
- A feature that addresses a symptom
- A solution that requires their product
- A dependency on their roadmap

What you can do:
- A config change that solves the root cause
- A one-line script that replaces a complex workaround
- An alias that saves you time without adding dependencies
- A mod that works with what you already have

## The Knowledge Mirror

a-hole's knowledge mirror does three things:

**1. Records your patterns**
- What you do every day
- What you do on every machine setup
- What you avoid (danger zones)
- What you ignore (dead weight)

**2. Identifies your earned knowledge**
- Configs you recreate on every machine
- Workflows you optimize
- Problems you solve
- Patterns you discover

**3. Exposes it for reuse**
- Browse by tool, by workflow, by similarity
- Preview as a diff before applying
- Apply with one command
- Revert with one command
- Share with the community

## The Community Layer

When you share a mod, you're not just sharing a diff. You're sharing:

- A problem someone else might have
- A solution that might work for them
- A pattern they might discover
- A workflow they might optimize

You're contributing to the commons of earned knowledge. You're helping someone else solve a problem they might have struggled with for months.

## Summary

Earned knowledge is:

1. **Self-created** — not told, not taught, discovered
2. **Durable** — can't be taken away from you
3. **Portable** — travels with you across machines, across tools
4. **Shareable** — helps others solve their problems
5. **Reversible** — can be removed without leaving traces
6. **Self-documenting** — the mod itself explains what it does
7. **Version-agnostic** — works against interfaces, not versions

Your knowledge is your most durable asset. Don't let it flow away with the telemetry. Keep it. Mirror it. Share it.

The only thing worth keeping is the intent. You articulate it clearly enough that it could survive being passed around.
